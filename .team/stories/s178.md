**User story.** As the PO, I want parallel stories to get distinct requirement ids, so that
their landings don't conflict in `docs/requirements` and nobody renumbers files by hand.

**What happened (2026-10-20 11:58, sprint 51).** s164, s165 and s166 each created
REQ-174 and REQ-175 in parallel (s164's developer later took 176/177 as well). s166 landed
first; s164's and s165's landings then conflicted in `docs/requirements`
(`decisions.md:540`). The repair allocated ids by hand: 174/175 to s166, 176/177 to s167,
then s165 182/183, s164 184/185, s162 186/187, s168 188/189
(`plans/mandate-2026-10-22.md:44`, `:52`; `reports/2026-10-22-stuck-story-repair.md:386`
lists the owed bug story).

**Cause (fact, read at 0e1e0991).** `requirements.next_id` is one above the highest number
among the files it was given (`requirements.py:403-408`), and `design_section` puts it in
the architect's brief (`requirements.py:448-452`, attached by
`dispatch._attach_requirements`, `dispatch.py:675-679`). A worktree step runs in a view
whose config is rooted in the story's worktree (`Runtime.get_story_view`,
`dispatch.py:519-527`; s161 renamed it from `Runtime.at`, and s161 is on main), so `sources(config)` reads that worktree's `docs/requirements`
(`requirements.py:475-479`), which starts as a copy of the sprint head. Every story forked
from the same head therefore sees the same highest number and is offered the same next id.
Nothing reserves an id across stories (`next_id` has no other caller).

**Placement:** Product Backlog. Files: `requirements.py`, `gates.py` (the design gate's
trace reasons), `dispatch.py` (the brief). Independent of the landing stories.
