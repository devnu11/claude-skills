**User story.** As the PO, I want a red story CI to tell me which job, step and lines
failed, so I can brief the developer at once instead of asking the liaison to fetch the log.

**What happened (2026-10-23, sprint 51).** s161's PR CI went red with one house-rules
finding (`compile_pattern` is not a verb). The hand-over named only the check; the liaison
fetched the job, step and lines and told the PO (`plans/mandate-2026-10-22.md:78`, `:84`).
The liaison wrote `scratchpad/ci_summary.sh <sha>` because this happened at every red
(`mandate-2026-10-22.md:85`).

**Cause (fact, read at 0e1e0991).** The story-CI poll reads the head's checks raw
(`StoryCiPoll.read_checks` calls `prs.list_checks`, `story_ci.py:162-167`) and records
them through `check_lines_of` (`story_ci.py:169-176`, `:179-181`). It never calls
`classify_check` (`ci.py:402-407`), which is what fills `Check.step` and `Check.lines`
from the job and its failed log; its only caller is the sprint boundary's `CiWatch`
(`ci.py:371`). So for story CI `step` and `lines` are empty, `_check_text` prints the bare
label (`ci.py:231-234`), and the `STORY_CI_RED` hand-over shows `{ci_checks}` as names
only (`handover.py:358-362`, `:445`). Even on the sprint path, `failing_lines` matches
pytest and Go patterns only (`FAILING_LINE`, `ci.py:42-47`) and falls back to the log's
tail (`ci.py:204-208`), so a ruff or house-rules failure shows whatever came last.

**Placement:** Product Backlog. Files: `story_ci.py`, `ci.py` (`FAILING_LINE`,
`classify_check` reuse), `handover.py`, the developer brief in `dispatch.py`. Watch the
GitHub API budget: classifying a check fetches its job and log, so do it once per new
`(sha, check)` and keep the 60 s poll gate (fixed in sprint 51).
