**User story.** As the human reading the dashboard, I want a chore row to mean work in
progress, so a "stop checks for 2 h" line is a stall I should look at, not a leftover.

**What happened (2026-10-20 and 2026-10-23, sprint 51).** The heartbeat showed "running
named tests for s164 for 18 min" with no pytest process running (`decisions.md:529`), and
after s162 landed the row "stop checks for developer-cli on s162" stayed open at 44 min and
then hours (`plans/mandate-2026-10-22.md:81`). Both are chores of a step that had ended.

**Cause (certain: the mechanism is read from the code at 0e1e0991; the interleaving is inferred from
the two live cases, not reproduced).** `Pulse.doing` saves the current chore, sets its own,
and on exit restores the saved one (`live.py:403-415`); the heartbeat has one `chore` value
(`live.py:536`). The callers run in different asyncio tasks or threads per story
(`stop_checks.py:170`, `runner_tools.py:174`, `dispatch.py:703`, `:1219`, `:1222`,
`:1433`, `:1801`, `po_tools.py:612`, `boundary_runner.py:252`). With parallel stories two
blocks overlap and don't exit last-in-first-out: A opens (saved: none), B opens (saved:
A), A exits (chore: none), B exits (chore: A). A's finished chore is then shown until the
next `doing` block ends cleanly. Stage rows (`showing`, `live.py:418-428`) remove
themselves by identity and don't have this problem.
