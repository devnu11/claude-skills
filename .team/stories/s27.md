title: A repeated bounce for the same reason escalates instead of looping
delivery: cli
acceptance:
  - id: AC1
    text: >-
      The runner records, for each bounce or hold, a normalised reason key (the gate
      or role's reason with ids, counts, timestamps, ports and paths stripped) and
      the role it went back to. When the same key repeats for a story (the repeat
      count is config, default 2), the runner does not send it round again.
  - id: AC2
    text: >-
      On a repeat, the runner escalates in code. It stops the loop, runs the Manager
      on the story with the repeated reason, the bounce history (which roles, which
      steps, the events) and the last job or gate output attached, and marks the story
      `needs_manager`. The Manager decides the owner of the fault (the role bounced
      to, another role, or the runner itself) and rules.
  - id: AC3
    text: >-
      The escalation counts as a round for none of the roles involved. A repeat caused
      by the runner (a failed prepare line, a gate that cannot pass) is flagged as a
      runner fault in the event and in the note to the human, and the PO is handed
      the story with that note.
  - id: AC4
    text: >-
      The normalisation and the repeat rule are data (a small table of patterns to
      strip), not branching. A different reason resets the count.
  - id: AC5
    text: >-
      `reference/protocol.md` (gates, rounds, escalation) and SKILL.md describe the
      rule. The dashboard flow and board show an escalated story as "needs manager"
      with the repeated reason. Tests cover a repeat, a changed reason, the Manager
      hand-over, the attached history and the zero-round rule. Coverage stays at or
      above 95%, and ruff check and ruff format are clean.

## User story

As the human, I want the runner to notice when a step keeps bouncing for the
same reason, and to hand it to the Manager, so that nobody burns rounds and money
going round a loop that the role cannot fix.

## Notes

Seen in sprint 8, events e451 to e458: the integration-tester and the
customer-proxy bounced s4 back and forth over a missing server. The cause was
the runner's own failed start, which neither role could fix. Human: "the
process needs to be fixed so no one ends up in a bone headed loop."
