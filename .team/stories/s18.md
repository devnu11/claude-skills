title: Reviewers can propose fixes as fixup commits, with role rules on fixup versus prose
delivery: cli
acceptance:
  - id: AC1
    text: >-
      The code-reviewer (and the quality-czar) may record a suggested fix as a fixup
      commit on the story (`fixup! <subject>`) instead of describing it in prose. The
      runner allows the write and commits it as a fixup, tagged with the reviewer's
      role and the defect id.
  - id: AC2
    text: >-
      A fixup suggestion still bounces the story. The developer who owns the step
      accepts it, reworks it or rejects it with a reason, and the runner squashes
      accepted fixups into the story's history. A rejected fixup is reverted, and the
      reason is recorded in the review file.
  - id: AC3
    text: >-
      The reviewer roles' rules include concrete examples of when to use which form.
      Fixup: a one-line mechanical fix such as s7's D4 (check the raw and the joined
      command), an escape that was missed, a renamed constant. Prose: a design-level
      problem, anything that needs a new or changed test, a choice between
      approaches, or a fix bigger than about 10 lines.
  - id: AC4
    text: >-
      `reference/protocol.md` (review step and scopes) and SKILL.md describe fixup
      suggestions. Tests cover the write permission, the fixup tagging, accept or
      squash, and reject or revert. Coverage stays at or above 95%, and ruff check and
      ruff format are clean.
---

## User story

As the human paying for the run, I want reviewers to hand over small fixes as
code instead of paragraphs, so that feedback costs fewer tokens and the fix is
exactly what the reviewer meant.

## Notes

Human, m9: "a reviewer making a suggestion via a fix up commit, is probably the
most token efficient way to record feedback. No security concerns. Would be
good to include in the role rules examples of when they should make suggestion
