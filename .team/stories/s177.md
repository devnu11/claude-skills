  - id: AC1

    text: >-
      When the sprint push is refused and the remote head still equals the runner's record
      (`pushed`), the landing holds at once with the refusal text as the hold reason. The
      merge is kept (`landed.merge` and `landed.pushed` stay set, the landing branch stays),
      no new merge is made, and `continue_story` / `release_story` retries the push of the
      same merge.
  - id: AC2
    environment: cli
    text: >-
      When the sprint push is refused but the fetched remote head equals the merge the
      runner tried to push, the landing is treated as pushed: the stack entry's `pushed` and
      `head` become the merge, the main checkout fast-forwards to it, and the story is
      recorded landed with `ci: green`, with no `landing dropped` event.
  - id: AC3
    environment: cli
    text: >-
      When the remote head descends from the record but is neither the record nor the merge
      (another story landed or a fix was pushed), the landing gives up its merge and lands
      again on the new head, as today; the hold "the sprint branch kept moving" is reached
      only after `MAX_RESTARTS` distinct remote heads, and its reason lists those heads.

**User story.** As the PO, I want a refused push to tell me why straight away, so a dead
remote or a bad lease doesn't cost five merges, five full checks and five CI runs before I
hear of it.

**What happened (2026-10-19 19:54, sprint 50).** The architect, reviewing s163 at e2e r1,
asked for a follow-up: "when a sprint push is refused and the remote didn't move, hold at
once with the refusal text, and treat a remote that equals the merge as a landed push. Today
both re-land up to 5 times and then hold with 'the sprint branch kept moving' (ADR-0084,
s163 known limits)". The PO relayed it as m284 (`outbox.jsonl`); the liaison deferred it
("m284 later", `decisions.md:525`). s163 is done and its AC3 is unchanged.

**Cause (fact, read at 0e1e0991).** `push_and_follow` calls `follow_remote` on any refusal
(`landing.py:504-513`). `follow_remote` first gives up the merge (`give_up(attempt,
GIVE_UP_REFUSED)`: `landed` cleared, landing branch deleted), fetches the sprint branch,
and sets `follows = is_ancestor(entry.pushed, remote)` (`landing.py:567-577`). That is true
when the remote equals `pushed` (it didn't move) and when it equals the merge (the push
went through but the reply was lost), so both cases return `None` and `run_attempts`
starts a fresh merge, up to `MAX_RESTARTS = 5` times, then holds with "the sprint branch
kept moving" (`landing.py:63`, `:238-247`).

**Placement:** Product Backlog. Files: `landing.py` (`follow_remote`, `push_and_follow`,
`run_attempts`) and the landing section of `reference/protocol.md` / ADR-0084. It is
independent of `gap-sprint-head-moved-story.md` (which changes what counts as a moved head,
not the refusal path), but both edit `landing.py`, so one team or an ordering.
