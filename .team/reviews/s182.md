## Findings (all open)

### F1 (defect, s182/AC3, REQ-219): a role's extra commit is blamed on the runner once folded

The code is as it was in the first pass (`story_rebase.py:262-267`). Say a role adds an
extra commit `Y` on top of the replayed commits and also leaves an in-scope edit. The rebase
is foldable, so `add_to_tip` amends `Y` into `Y'` and `_add_leftovers` records `Y'` in
`rebase.made`. The refusal then reads `- extra Y' … (made by the runner)`. But the role made
that commit, so this is the s180 mix-up again.

Fix: put back the 9bc5c21d guard under a name with a house-rules verb (for example
`is_tip_replayed`). Update the three fold tests' scripted replies for the extra `log` calls;
don't remove the guard. Also add a test where a role-made extra tip plus an in-scope
leftover gives the dirty refusal naming the file, or `made by the role`, and never
`made by the runner`. The fold tests are in `test_s182_story_rebase.py` and
`test_story_rebase_steps.py`.

### F2 (test gap, s182/AC1, AC2, REQ-217, REQ-218): no real-git test of the settle

`tests/e2e/` still has no s182 test. The story's Tooling section promises one, and so does
the design checklist ("real git in a scratch repo"). These are still only proven against
`FakeSystem` argv order:
- `reset --keep HEAD^` after `guard.set_aside` removes a tracked and an untracked
  out-of-scope file and keeps the unstaged in-scope edit;
- `refs/agile-team/set-aside/<sha>` holds the set-aside edit;
- `add_to_tip` keeps the tip's subject and author and leaves a clean tree;
- `_settle_step_tree` followed by `_apply_rebase` adopts the branch, so `find_refusal` sees
  the settle's output. That is the exact path that failed for s180.

### F3 (test gap, s182/AC4, REQ-221): renumbering inside the replayed commits is untested

No test has a resolution that changes REQ/ADR ids in the conflicting files through
`rebase --continue` and then checks that `find_refusal` passes and the branch is adopted.

### F4 (test gap, s182/AC3, REQ-220): the advance refusal is only unit-tested

`Pipeline.ruling_move_refusal` has good unit coverage. But no test applies an `advance`
ruling through the dispatch path to a story with a queued rebase, then checks that
`story.step` and `story.rebase` are unchanged and that the next pick is the same rebase.
