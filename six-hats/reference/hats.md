# The six hats

Edward de Bono, *Six Thinking Hats* (1985). Each hat is a mode of thinking
everyone in the room uses at the same time ("parallel thinking"), instead of
arguing from fixed positions.

| Hat | Mode | Covers | Must not |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | Information | Facts, figures, what is known, what is missing, how to get it | Interpret, argue or judge |
| Red | Feelings | Gut reactions, intuition, likes, dislikes, fears | Justify the feeling |
| Black | Caution | Risks, weaknesses, why it may fail, conflicts with constraints | Propose fixes or turn into pessimism without a reason |
| Yellow | Benefits | Value, best realistic case, why it could work | Hedge with "but" |
| Green | Creativity | Alternatives, new ideas, ways round Black's risks, provocations | Evaluate ideas while generating them |
| Blue | Process | Framing the question, choosing the sequence, summarising, next steps | Contribute content in place of the other hats |

## Prompting questions

**White**
- What do we know for certain? What do we only believe?
- What information is missing, and how would we get it?
- Whose numbers are these, and how current are they?

**Red**
- What is your first reaction, in one word?
- Which option feels right, and which makes you uneasy?
- How will the people affected feel about it?

**Black**
- What could go wrong, and how likely and how bad is each?
- Which constraint or past experience does this conflict with?
- What is the cost of being wrong, and can it be undone?

**Yellow**
- What is the best realistic outcome?
- Who benefits, and how?
- Why might this work even where it looks weak?

**Green**
- What else could we do, including doing nothing or doing it later?
- How could we remove or shrink each Black risk?
- What would we do if the main constraint did not exist?

**Blue**
- What exactly are we deciding, and by when?
- Which hat do we need next?
- What did we conclude, and what happens now?
