# six-hats

Parallel thinking: look at the problem through one hat at a time, never
mixing them. Each hat is a mode, not a person or a verdict. What each hat
covers, what it must not do and the questions it asks are in
`reference/hats.md` next to this file. Read it before the first hat.

## Steps

1. **Blue: frame it.** State the decision as one question, its constraints and
   what "decided" looks like. If the question is vague, ask until it is one
   sentence. Pick a sequence from `reference/sequences.md` and say which.
2. **Pick the mode.** *Solo*: you wear every hat on the user's facts.
   *Facilitated*: for each hat, give your points, then ask the user for theirs
   before moving on. Default to solo unless the user wants to take part or
   holds facts you don't.
3. **Run the hats in order.** One heading per hat. Stay strictly in that hat;
   anything that belongs to another hat is parked and raised there.
4. **Blue: close it.** Summarise in the format at the end of
   `reference/sequences.md`: the decision or recommendation, the risks that
   carry over with how to handle each, and the open questions with who
   answers them.

## Rules

- **One hat at a time.** No "but" in Yellow, no fixes in Black, no
  justification in Red.
- **Yellow before Black** when judging an idea, so it isn't killed before its
  value is stated.
- **Red is short:** gut reactions in a line or two each, and label them as
  feelings.
- **White marks its certainty.** Separate known facts from assumptions, and
  list the facts still missing as questions.
- **Green needs alternatives.** At least three options, including one the
  user did not bring up.
- Don't decide for the user on value judgements; recommend, and say which hat
  the recommendation rests on.
